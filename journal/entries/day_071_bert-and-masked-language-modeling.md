## 🧠 Concept Explanation

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) learns deep contextual word vectors by training on a masked language modeling (MLM) objective. Unlike left‑to‑right models, BERT sees the whole sequence at once. During pre‑training, 15 % of tokens are randomly masked: 80 % become a special [MASK] token, 10 % are replaced by a random token, and 10 % stay unchanged. The encoder must predict each original token using bidirectional context, forcing it to fuse syntax and semantics from both directions. A second task, next‑sentence prediction (NSP), teaches sentence‑level relationships via the [CLS] token. BERT stacks 12 (base) or 24 (large) identical encoder layers, each with multi‑head self‑attention, a feed‑forward network, residual connections, and layer normalization. Positional embeddings are added to token embeddings so the model knows token order despite permutation‑invariant attention. The MLM loss is a cross‑entropy over the vocabulary for every masked position; the NSP loss is binary classification on the [CLS] representation. After pre‑training, BERT is fine‑tuned on downstream tasks (classification, QA, NER) by adding a task‑specific head and updating all parameters, allowing the rich linguistic knowledge to adapt with relatively little data. The bidirectional attention lets each token attend to all others, capturing long‑range dependencies that unidirectional models miss, which is why BERT sets strong baselines on GLUE, SQuAD, and many other benchmarks.

## ✅ Key Points

- Bidirectional Transformer encoder trained with masked language modeling.
- 15% tokens masked; 80% become [MASK], 10% random, 10% unchanged.
- Two pre‑training objectives: MLM (token prediction) and NSP (sentence pair classification).
- Stack of 12 (base) or 24 (large) identical encoder layers with multi‑head self‑attention.
- Fine‑tuning adds a task‑specific head and updates all parameters for downstream tasks.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

BERT is like a detective who reads an entire mystery novel with some words blacked out, then uses the surrounding story on both sides to infer each missing word.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Treating BERT as a static embedding lookup instead of fine‑tuning the whole model.
- Assuming the [CLS] token always yields the best sentence representation for every task.
- Ignoring the 15% masking ratio and using a different masking strategy without adjusting learning rates.
