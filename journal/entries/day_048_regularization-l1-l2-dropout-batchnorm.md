## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Regularization is the set of techniques used to prevent overfitting by constraining model complexity. L1 regularization (Lasso) adds the sum of absolute weight values to the loss, driving many weights exactly to zero and performing automatic feature selection. L2 regularization (Ridge) adds the sum of squared weights, encouraging small, distributed weights without sparsity. Dropout randomly deactivates a fraction of neurons during each training step, forcing the network to learn redundant representations and reducing co-adaptation. Batch Normalization normalizes layer inputs to zero mean and unit variance per mini-batch, then learns scale and shift parameters; this stabilizes training, allows higher learning rates, and acts as a mild regularizer through batch-statistic noise. Together, these methods address different overfitting mechanisms: weight magnitude (L1/L2), architectural redundancy (Dropout), and internal covariate shift (BatchNorm). In practice, L2 and Dropout are standard in fully-connected and convolutional layers, while BatchNorm is ubiquitous in deep CNNs and Transformers. L1 is preferred when interpretability or explicit feature selection is needed. Combining them requires care: BatchNorm's scaling can diminish L2's effect, and Dropout after BatchNorm may interfere with normalization statistics. A typical pipeline applies BatchNorm -> Activation -> Dropout -> next layer, with L2 weight decay on all learnable parameters.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- L1 (Lasso) induces sparsity by zeroing weights; L2 (Ridge) shrinks weights smoothly.
- Dropout breaks co-adaptation by randomly masking activations during training.
- BatchNorm stabilizes learning by normalizing layer inputs per mini-batch.
- BatchNorm provides slight regularization via batch-statistic noise.
- Order matters: BatchNorm -> Activation -> Dropout is a common effective pattern.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Regularization is like a teacher forcing students to study all chapters (L2), skip memorizing exact sentences (Dropout), and take practice quizzes under varying conditions (BatchNorm) so they generalize to the final exam instead of overfitting to homework.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Applying Dropout after BatchNorm without adjusting momentum, corrupting running statistics.
- Using high L2 weight decay with BatchNorm, which reduces BatchNorm's effective regularization.
- Forgetting to switch Dropout/BatchNorm to eval mode during inference, causing stochastic outputs.
