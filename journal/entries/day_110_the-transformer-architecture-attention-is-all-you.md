## 🧠 Concept Explanation

The Transformer, introduced in "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017), replaces recurrence and convolution with a pure attention mechanism. At its core is scaled dot‑product self‑attention: for each token we compute query, key, and value vectors, then weight the values by the compatibility of queries and keys. This lets every position attend to all others in parallel, capturing long‑range dependencies without sequential processing. Multi‑head attention runs several such attention layers in parallel, each learning different relational patterns (e.g., syntactic vs. semantic). The encoder consists of N identical layers, each containing a multi‑head self‑attention sub‑layer followed by a position‑wise feed‑forward network, both wrapped in residual connections and layer normalization. The decoder mirrors the encoder but adds a second multi‑head attention that attends to the encoder output (cross‑attention) and uses masked self‑attention to prevent looking at future tokens. Positional encodings (sinusoidal or learned) inject sequence order information, since attention itself is permutation‑invariant. The model is trained end‑to‑end with teacher forcing and label smoothing, achieving state‑of‑the‑art results on translation and many downstream tasks.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Self‑attention computes contextual representations by weighting all tokens simultaneously.
- Multi‑head attention splits the representation space to capture diverse relationships.
- Encoder‑decoder stacks use residual connections and layer norm for stable deep training.
- Positional encodings provide order information absent from pure attention.
- Masked self‑attention in the decoder enforces autoregressive generation.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Imagine a team of translators where each member simultaneously reads the whole source sentence, highlights the words most relevant to their assigned phrase, and then writes their part — no one waits for the previous translator to finish.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to mask future tokens in decoder self‑attention, causing information leakage.
- Omitting positional encodings, which makes the model treat sequences as bags of words.
- Using a single attention head instead of multi‑head, limiting the model's ability to capture varied dependencies.
