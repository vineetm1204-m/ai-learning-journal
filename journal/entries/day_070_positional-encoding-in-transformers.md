# Day 70 — Positional encoding in Transformers

> 📅 September 16, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Transformers process tokens in parallel, losing inherent sequence order. Positional encoding injects positional information into token embeddings so the model can distinguish "dog bites man" from "man bites dog." The original "Attention Is All You Need" paper uses sinusoidal functions of different frequencies: PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model)) and PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model)). This choice allows the model to easily learn relative positions because PE(pos+k) can be expressed as a linear function of PE(pos). The wavelengths form a geometric progression from 2π to 10000·2π, covering a wide range of contexts. The encodings are added to token embeddings (same dimension) before the first attention layer. Because they are deterministic and not learned, they generalize to sequence lengths longer than those seen during training. Learned positional embeddings are an alternative but may not extrapolate as well. The key insight is that position is not a separate input but a continuous signal mixed with content, enabling the self-attention mechanism to weigh tokens based on both what they are and where they appear.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Transformers are permutation-invariant without positional encoding.
- Sinusoidal encoding uses fixed sine/cosine waves of geometrically increasing wavelengths.
- Encodings are added to token embeddings, not concatenated, preserving dimension.
- The formulation allows linear extrapolation to unseen sequence lengths.
- Relative position information is linearly decodable from absolute encodings.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Like giving each musician in an orchestra a seat number so the conductor knows who plays when, even though they all read the same score simultaneously.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing positional encoding with learned positional embeddings and assuming they behave identically.
- Thinking the encoding is concatenated (doubling dimension) instead of added.
- Believing sinusoidal encoding only works for fixed maximum sequence length.
