## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Regularization is a set of techniques used to prevent overfitting in machine learning models by adding constraints or noise during training. L1 and L2 regularization add penalty terms to the loss function proportional to the absolute (L1) or squared (L2) magnitude of weights, encouraging smaller weights and simpler models. L1 tends to produce sparse weights, effectively performing feature selection, while L2 shrinks weights uniformly. Dropout randomly deactivates a fraction of neurons during each training step, forcing the network to learn redundant representations and reducing co-adaptation. Batch Normalization normalizes layer inputs to have zero mean and unit variance, stabilizing training and acting as a regularizer by adding noise through batch statistics. Together, these methods improve generalization by limiting model complexity, reducing reliance on specific neurons, and smoothing the optimization landscape. Proper tuning of regularization strength (lambda for L1/L2, dropout rate) is crucial: too little leads to overfitting, too much causes underfitting. In practice, combinations like L2 with dropout and BatchNorm are common in deep architectures. Regularization addresses the bias-variance tradeoff by intentionally increasing bias to reduce variance. L1 regularization (Lasso) adds the sum of absolute weights, driving some weights to exactly zero, which is useful for interpretability. L2 regularization (Ridge) adds the sum of squared weights, preventing any single weight from dominating. Dropout, with probability p, zeros out activations, effectively training an ensemble of thinned networks. BatchNorm computes mean and variance per mini-batch, then scales and shifts with learnable parameters, allowing higher learning rates and reducing internal covariate shift. These techniques are not mutually exclusive; they complement each other. For instance, BatchNorm reduces the need for dropout in some layers, but they can be combined. Monitoring validation loss helps select hyperparameters.

## ✅ Key Points

- L1 regularization adds λΣ|w| to loss, promoting sparsity and feature selection.
- L2 regularization adds λΣw² to loss, shrinking weights uniformly and preventing large values.
- Dropout randomly zeros activations with probability p during training, creating an implicit ensemble.
- BatchNorm normalizes layer inputs per mini-batch, stabilizing training and providing regularization via batch statistics noise.
- Combining regularizers (e.g., L2 + Dropout + BatchNorm) often yields better generalization than any single method.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Setting regularization strength too high, causing underfitting and high bias.
- Applying dropout at inference time instead of scaling weights (or using inverted dropout).
- Using BatchNorm with very small batch sizes, leading to noisy statistics and unstable training.
