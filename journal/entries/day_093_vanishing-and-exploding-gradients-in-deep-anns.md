# Day 93 — Vanishing and exploding gradients in deep ANNs

> 📅 October 22, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

In deep artificial neural networks, gradients are computed by back‑propagating the loss from the output layer to the input layer. At each layer the gradient is multiplied by the layer's weight matrix and the derivative of its activation function. If the weights are small (e.g., initialized near zero) or the activation derivatives are bounded by values < 1 (as with sigmoid or tanh), repeated multiplication causes the gradient magnitude to shrink exponentially — the vanishing gradient problem. Conversely, large weights or activation derivatives > 1 (possible with ReLU when inputs are large) make the gradient grow exponentially — the exploding gradient problem. Both phenomena prevent effective learning: vanishing gradients stall weight updates in early layers, while exploding gradients cause unstable, divergent updates. Modern solutions include careful weight initialization (Xavier/Glorot, He), batch normalization, residual connections that add identity shortcuts, and using activation functions with non‑saturating derivatives such as ReLU or its variants. Gradient clipping is a practical safeguard against explosion during training.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Gradients are products of weight matrices and activation derivatives across layers.
- Repeated multiplication by values < 1 leads to vanishing gradients; > 1 leads to exploding gradients.
- Sigmoid/tanh saturate, causing derivatives near zero; ReLU avoids saturation for positive inputs.
- Proper initialization (Xavier, He) keeps gradient scale near 1 at start of training.
- Residual connections and batch normalization provide direct gradient pathways and stabilize scale.

## 🏗️ Diagram / Formula

```
Layer L:  x_L = σ(W_L x_{L-1} + b_L)
Backprop:  δ_{L-1} = (W_L^T δ_L) ⊙ σ'(W_L x_{L-1} + b_L)
Gradient magnitude ≈ ∏_{k=1}^{L} ‖W_k‖ · σ'(·)
```

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Imagine a game of telephone where each person whispers the message multiplied by a factor; if the factor is <1 the message fades to silence, if >1 it blows up into noise.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using sigmoid/tanh in very deep networks without residual links.
- Ignoring weight initialization and relying on default random values.
- Training without gradient clipping when using recurrent or very deep architectures.
