# Day 90 — Learning rate schedules: step decay, cosine annealing, warm restarts

> 📅 October 19, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Learning rate schedules dynamically adjust the step size during training so the optimizer can make large updates early and fine‑tune later. Step decay reduces the rate by a fixed factor every N epochs, giving a piecewise‑constant curve that is easy to implement but can cause abrupt changes. Cosine annealing follows a smooth cosine curve from an initial maximum down to a minimum, providing a gradual decay that often yields better convergence because the optimizer never experiences a sudden jump. Warm restarts (SGDR) periodically reset the learning rate to its maximum and then anneal again, allowing the model to escape shallow minima and explore new regions of the loss landscape. All three strategies share the goal of balancing exploration (high LR) and exploitation (low LR) without manual tuning at every epoch. In practice, cosine annealing with warm restarts often outperforms simple step decay on deep networks, especially when combined with a short warm‑up phase that linearly increases the LR from a tiny value to the starting maximum, stabilizing early training.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Step decay: multiply LR by a factor (e.g., 0.1) at predefined epoch intervals.
- Cosine annealing: LR follows lr_min + 0.5*(lr_max-lr_min)*(1+cos(pi*epoch/T_max)).
- Warm restarts (SGDR): periodically reset LR to lr_max and re‑anneal, enabling escape from local minima.
- Warm‑up phase: linearly increase LR for the first few epochs to avoid instability.
- Schedules trade off exploration (high LR) vs. exploitation (low LR) automatically.

## 🏗️ Diagram / Formula

```
lr(t) = lr_min + 0.5*(lr_max - lr_min)*(1 + cos(pi * t / T_max))

Step decay: lr = lr_0 * gamma^{floor(epoch / step_size)}

Warm restart: repeat cosine cycle every T_0 epochs, T_i = T_0 * T_mult^i
```

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Like a hiker descending a mountain: start with big strides (high LR), gradually shorten steps (cosine decay), and occasionally climb back up to a ridge (warm restart) to find a better path down.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using a fixed LR for all epochs, causing either divergence or extremely slow convergence.
- Setting step decay intervals too short, leading to premature LR drops before the model has learned useful features.
- Skipping warm‑up when training very deep networks, resulting in unstable early gradients.
