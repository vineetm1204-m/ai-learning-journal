## 🧠 Concept Explanation

GPT (Generative Pre‑trained Transformer) is a family of large language models that learn to predict the next token in a sequence, a paradigm known as autoregressive language modeling. During pre‑training the model sees massive text corpora and is trained with a causal (masked) self‑attention mechanism that prevents each position from attending to future tokens, ensuring the prediction at time t depends only on tokens 1…t‑1. The architecture consists of a stack of Transformer decoder blocks: each block contains a masked multi‑head self‑attention layer followed by a position‑wise feed‑forward network, with residual connections and layer normalization. Because the model factorizes the joint probability of a sequence into a product of conditional probabilities, it can generate coherent text by sampling token‑by‑token. Fine‑tuning on downstream tasks (e.g., instruction following, code generation) adapts the same autoregressive objective to specific domains. The key strengths are scalability — performance improves predictably with model size, data, and compute — and the ability to perform in‑context learning, where a few examples in the prompt steer the model’s behavior without weight updates.

## ✅ Key Points

- Autoregressive factorization: P(x) = ∏ₜ P(xₜ | x₁…xₜ₋₁).
- Causal masked self‑attention prevents leakage of future tokens.
- Stack of Transformer decoder layers with residual connections and layer norm.
- Pre‑training on massive corpora followed by task‑specific fine‑tuning or prompting.
- Scaling laws: loss decreases smoothly as parameters, data, and compute increase.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Writing a story one word at a time, where each new word is chosen based only on the words already written, never peeking at the ending.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Treating GPT as a bidirectional encoder (like BERT) and expecting it to use future context during generation.
- Ignoring the need for proper token‑level masking during training, leading to information leakage and inflated perplexity.
- Assuming larger models automatically solve all tasks without adequate prompt engineering or fine‑tuning.
