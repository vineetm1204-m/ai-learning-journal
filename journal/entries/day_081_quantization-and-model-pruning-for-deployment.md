## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Quantization and pruning are two complementary techniques that shrink a trained neural network so it can run faster and with less memory on edge devices or servers. Quantization reduces the numerical precision of weights and activations — typically from 32‑bit floating point to 8‑bit integers — which cuts memory bandwidth and enables hardware‑accelerated integer arithmetic. There are two main flavors: post‑training quantization, which calibrates a frozen model using a small calibration set, and quantization‑aware training, which simulates low‑precision effects during training to recover accuracy. Pruning, on the other hand, removes unnecessary parameters. Structured pruning deletes entire filters, channels, or layers, preserving the regular tensor shapes that hardware expects, while unstructured pruning zeroes out individual weights, requiring sparse‑matrix support. Both methods can be combined: a model is first pruned to reduce the parameter count, then quantized to lower the bit‑width. The trade‑off is accuracy versus efficiency; careful calibration, fine‑tuning, and evaluation on the target hardware are essential to avoid catastrophic degradation. Modern frameworks such as TensorFlow Lite, ONNX Runtime, and PyTorch provide built‑in pipelines for quantization‑aware training and structured pruning, making deployment‑ready models accessible to practitioners.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Quantization lowers precision (e.g., FP32 → INT8) to reduce memory and compute cost.
- Post‑training quantization is fast but may lose accuracy; quantization‑aware training mitigates this.
- Structured pruning removes whole filters/channels, keeping dense tensor layouts for hardware.
- Unstructured pruning creates sparsity but needs specialized sparse kernels for speedups.
- Combining pruning then quantization often yields the best size‑latency‑accuracy trade‑off.

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Quantization is like switching from a high‑resolution photo to a compressed JPEG — smaller file, slightly less detail. Pruning is like removing blank pages from a book before binding it, making the book lighter without losing the story.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Applying post‑training quantization without a calibration dataset, causing large accuracy drops.
- Pruning too aggressively in one step and skipping fine‑tuning, leading to unrecoverable performance loss.
- Assuming unstructured sparsity automatically speeds up inference on all hardware; many accelerators need structured sparsity.
