# Day 88 — Weight initialization strategies: Xavier, He, random

> 📅 October 15, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Weight initialization sets the starting values of a neural network's parameters before training begins. If weights are too large, activations explode and gradients vanish or blow up; if they are too small, signals shrink and learning stalls. Xavier (Glorot) initialization assumes a linear activation and keeps the variance of activations and back‑propagated gradients equal across layers by drawing weights from a distribution with variance 2/(n_in + n_out). He initialization adapts this for ReLU‑type nonlinearities, where half the units are zeroed out, so it uses variance 2/n_in (or 2/n_out for the backward pass). Random uniform or Gaussian draws with these variances are common; the uniform range is ±√(6/(n_in+n_out)) for Xavier and ±√(6/n_in) for He. Proper initialization places the network in a regime where gradients flow healthily, enabling faster convergence and reducing sensitivity to learning‑rate choice. Modern libraries default to He for layers followed by ReLU and Xavier for tanh/sigmoid, but practitioners should match the scheme to their activation functions and architecture depth.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Xavier keeps forward and backward variance balanced for symmetric activations (tanh, sigmoid).
- He scales variance by 2/n_in to compensate for ReLU's zero‑half behavior.
- Both methods can use Gaussian or uniform distributions with analytically derived bounds.
- Mismatched initialization (e.g., Xavier with ReLU) leads to vanishing/exploding gradients.
- Default initializers in frameworks follow these guidelines but can be overridden.

## 🏗️ Diagram / Formula

```
Xavier (uniform):  W ~ U(-√(6/(n_in+n_out)), √(6/(n_in+n_out)))
He (uniform):      W ~ U(-√(6/n_in), √(6/n_in))
Xavier (normal):   W ~ N(0, √(2/(n_in+n_out)))
He (normal):       W ~ N(0, √(2/n_in))
```

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Choosing weight initialization is like tuning the tension of guitar strings before a song: too loose and the notes fade (vanishing gradients), too tight and they snap (exploding gradients); the right tension lets the music flow smoothly from the first chord.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using Xavier initialization with ReLU layers, causing dead neurons early in training.
- Leaving all weights at zero or a constant value, which makes every neuron compute identical updates.
- Ignoring fan‑in/fan‑out differences in asymmetric layers (e.g., convolutions) and applying a one‑size‑fits‑all variance.
