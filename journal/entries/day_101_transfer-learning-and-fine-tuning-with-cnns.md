# Day 101 — Transfer learning and fine-tuning with CNNs

> 📅 October 30, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Transfer learning leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) that has already learned generic visual features—edges, textures, shapes—on a large dataset such as ImageNet. Instead of training a new network from scratch, we reuse the pretrained backbone and adapt it to a target task with far fewer labeled examples. The typical workflow freezes the early layers, which capture low‑level patterns that transfer well across domains, and replaces the final classification head with a new fully‑connected layer (or a small sub‑network) suited to the target classes. During fine‑tuning we then unfreeze a subset of the higher layers and continue training with a low learning rate, allowing the model to specialize its mid‑level representations while preserving the robust low‑level filters. This approach dramatically reduces training time, data requirements, and the risk of overfitting, especially when the target dataset is small or similar to the source domain. Careful choices—how many layers to freeze, learning‑rate schedule, and whether to use a global average pooling versus a dense head—determine the final performance.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Pretrained CNN provides reusable low‑level feature extractors.
- Freeze early layers; replace the classification head for the new task.
- Fine‑tune higher layers with a small learning rate to adapt mid‑level features.
- Greatly cuts data and compute needs; mitigates overfitting on small datasets.
- Decisions on freeze depth, head design, and LR schedule strongly affect results.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Freezing too many layers, preventing the model from adapting to domain‑specific features.
- Using a high learning rate during fine‑tuning, which destroys the pretrained weights.
- Ignoring the mismatch between source and target label spaces, leading to an ill‑shaped classification head.
