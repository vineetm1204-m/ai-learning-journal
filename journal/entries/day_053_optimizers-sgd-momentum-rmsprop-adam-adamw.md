# Day 53 — Optimizers: SGD, Momentum, RMSProp, Adam, AdamW

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Optimizers guide how a neural network updates its weights to minimize loss. Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) takes small steps in the direction of the negative gradient, but it can oscillate in ravines and get stuck in local minima. Momentum adds a velocity term that accumulates past gradients, smoothing the trajectory and accelerating convergence along consistent directions. RMSProp adapts the learning rate per parameter by dividing by a running average of recent gradient magnitudes, which helps in non-stationary settings. Adam combines Momentum and RMSProp: it maintains both a first-moment estimate (velocity) and a second-moment estimate (adaptive learning rates), with bias correction for the initial steps. AdamW improves Adam by decoupling weight decay from the adaptive learning rate, applying weight decay directly to the weights rather than the gradient, which yields better generalization. Choosing an optimizer depends on the problem: SGD with momentum often generalizes best for large-scale vision tasks with careful tuning, while Adam/AdamW are robust defaults for transformers, NLP, and problems with sparse gradients. Learning rate scheduling, warmup, and proper weight decay are as important as the optimizer choice itself.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- SGD updates weights by subtracting gradient scaled by learning rate; simple but slow in ravines.
- Momentum accumulates an exponential moving average of gradients to accelerate consistent directions.
- RMSProp adapts per-parameter learning rates using a moving average of squared gradients.
- Adam combines Momentum and RMSProp with bias correction for fast, adaptive convergence.
- AdamW decouples weight decay from adaptive steps, improving generalization over Adam.

## 🏗️ Diagram / Formula

```
SGD:       w = w - lr * g
Momentum:  v = beta * v + (1-beta) * g; w = w - lr * v
RMSProp:   s = gamma * s + (1-gamma) * g^2; w = w - lr * g / sqrt(s + eps)
Adam:      m = beta1 * m + (1-beta1) * g; v = beta2 * v + (1-beta2) * g^2
           m_hat = m / (1-beta1^t); v_hat = v / (1-beta2^t)
           w = w - lr * m_hat / (sqrt(v_hat) + eps)
AdamW:     w = w - lr * (m_hat / (sqrt(v_hat) + eps) + weight_decay * w)
```

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

SGD is like walking downhill blindly; Momentum adds a heavy ball that rolls through small bumps; RMSProp adjusts step size per terrain steepness; Adam is a smart hiker with momentum and adaptive steps; AdamW ensures the hiker doesn't overpack (weight decay) regardless of terrain.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using default learning rates without tuning; Adam's default 1e-3 may be too high for some architectures.
- Applying weight decay incorrectly in Adam (coupled) instead of AdamW (decoupled), hurting generalization.
- Ignoring learning rate schedules or warmup, especially for transformers and large models.
