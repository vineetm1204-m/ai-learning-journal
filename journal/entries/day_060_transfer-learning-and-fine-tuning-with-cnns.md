# Day 60 — Transfer learning and fine-tuning with CNNs

> 📅 August 29, 2026

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Transfer learning leverages knowledge from a model trained on a large source dataset (like ImageNet) to solve a related target task with limited data. In CNNs, early layers learn generic features (edges, textures) while deeper layers capture task-specific patterns. We typically freeze the early "backbone" layers and replace the final classification head with new layers suited to our classes. Fine-tuning goes further: after initial training of the new head, we unfreeze some or all backbone layers and continue training with a very low learning rate. This adapts the pre-trained features to the target domain without catastrophic forgetting. The key insight is that pre-trained weights provide a far better initialization than random weights, dramatically reducing data requirements and training time. For example, a ResNet-50 trained on 1.2M images can be adapted to a 10-class medical imaging task with only hundreds of images. Success depends on domain similarity (natural images to medical scans works better than to spectrograms), dataset size (smaller datasets favor more freezing), and careful learning rate scheduling. Modern practices include using adaptive optimizers, layer-wise learning rate decay, and progressive unfreezing.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Pre-trained CNNs provide hierarchical feature extractors: generic low-level features transfer well across vision tasks.
- Freeze backbone initially; train only the new task-specific head to avoid destroying useful features.
- Fine-tune with low learning rates (1e-4 to 1e-5) and unfreeze layers progressively from top to bottom.
- Domain similarity dictates transferability: natural-to-medical works better than natural-to-satellite.
- Smaller target datasets require more freezing; larger datasets allow deeper fine-tuning.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using a high learning rate during fine-tuning, which destroys pre-trained features (catastrophic forgetting).
- Freezing all backbone layers when the target domain differs significantly (e.g., natural images to sketches), preventing adaptation.
- Ignoring preprocessing mismatch: applying ImageNet normalization (mean=[0.485,0.456,0.406], std=[0.229,0.224,0.225]) to non-RGB or differently scaled inputs.
