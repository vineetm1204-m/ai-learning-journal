# Day 49 — Learning rate schedules: step decay, cosine annealing, warm restarts

> 📅 August 15, 2026

---

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

Learning rate schedules dynamically adjust the step size during training to balance fast initial convergence with fine-grained optimization later. A constant learning rate often leads to oscillations near minima or premature stagnation. Step decay reduces the rate by a fixed factor (e.g., 0.1) at predefined epochs, providing a simple but effective staircase descent. Cosine annealing follows a cosine curve, smoothly decreasing the learning rate from a maximum to a minimum over a cycle, allowing the optimizer to explore broadly early and settle precisely later. Warm restarts (SGDR) extend cosine annealing by periodically resetting the learning rate to its maximum, enabling the model to escape sharp local minima and explore wider, flatter basins associated with better generalization. These schedules mimic a teacher who starts with broad strokes and gradually refines details, occasionally revisiting the big picture to avoid tunnel vision. Proper scheduling often matters more than the initial learning rate value itself, and modern practitioners frequently combine warmup (gradual increase from near-zero) with cosine decay for stable training of deep networks like Transformers.

---

## ✅ Key Points

- Step decay drops LR by a factor at fixed intervals; simple but requires manual epoch tuning.
- Cosine annealing smoothly decays LR following a half-cosine wave, no sharp transitions.
- Warm restarts (SGDR) periodically reset LR to max, promoting escape from sharp minima.
- Warmup phases (linear/constant increase) prevent early instability, especially with large batches.
- Schedule choice interacts with optimizer (SGD vs Adam) and architecture; cosine+warmup is standard for Transformers.

## 🏗️ Diagram / Formula

```
Cosine Annealing Formula:

lr_t = lr_min + 0.5 * (lr_max - lr_min) * (1 + cos(pi * t / T))

Where:
  t = current epoch/step
  T = total epochs/steps in cycle
  lr_max = initial learning rate
  lr_min = minimum learning rate (often 0 or 1e-6)

Warm Restart: Repeat above with T_i = T_0 * T_mult^i after each cycle.
```

## 💡 Real-World Analogy

Like a hiker descending a mountain: step decay takes deliberate platform breaks, cosine annealing follows a smooth winding path, and warm restarts occasionally climb back up to a higher ridge to find a better valley.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using a constant learning rate for all epochs, causing divergence or poor convergence.
- Setting step decay milestones arbitrarily without validation monitoring.
- Skipping warmup when training large models (e.g., Transformers) with large batch sizes, leading to early instability.
